## 1. Regulatory drivers (timing hooks)

### CBN Baseline Standards for Automated AML Solutions
- Circular BSD/DIR/PUB/LAB/019/002, issued 10 March 2026. [N1, N2, N3]
- Implementation roadmap due within 3 months (about 10 June 2026). This has passed. No public reporting found on who met it or on any extension. [N1, N3]
- Full compliance: deposit money banks by 10 September 2027 (18 months). **Fintechs, PSPs, MMOs and other institutions by 10 March 2028 (24 months).** [N3, N4]
- Vendors are marketing directly against it: Smartcomply says Adhere is aligned with the standards [N15]; Youverify says the circular "mandates certified automated AML solutions" [vendor claim, N4].

### CBN Circular PSS/DIR/PUB/CIR/001/004 (15 June 2026)
- Title: market structure requirements, data localisation, ultimate beneficial ownership disclosure and systemic oversight in the payments system. Addressed to DMBs, MFBs, MMOs, switches, PTSPs, PSSPs, Super Agents and other licensed operators. [N10]
- UBO: institutions with digital payment operations must disclose the ultimate beneficial owners of significant shareholders and keep the records current. [N9, N10]
- **Data localisation: all payment transaction data generated in Nigeria must be stored and managed in Nigeria by 1 January 2027.** [N10, N11]
- Market-share caps on issuing and acquiring (sources disagree on the exact thresholds). Monthly market-share reporting, full compliance by 31 December 2026 (one law firm). [N10, N11]

### AML/CFT/CPF Regulations 2022 and reporting
- The CBN AML/CFT/CPF Regulations 2022 cover PSPs, switches and other fintechs. [N5]
- STRs go to the NFIU "immediately" per the regulation text. Secondary sources give 24 hours (terrorism), 5 working days or 7 days, through goAML. [N5, secondary]
- CTR thresholds conflict across sources: NGN 5m (individuals) and NGN 10m (corporates), commonly attributed to the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022, versus USD thresholds in the CBN regulation text. Check before use. [N5, secondary]
- Tiered KYC applies. One 2026 source says BVN or NIN verification is now required at all tiers (unconfirmed). [secondary]

### Enforcement history
- April 2024: CBN placed a temporary customer-onboarding ban on several fintechs over KYC failures. Reports name Kuda and PalmPay; some lists add OPay, Moniepoint and Paga. Details differ by source. [N7, N8]
- 2024: reported NGN 1bn fines each on Moniepoint and OPay for KYC non-compliance. OPay denied it. [N6]
- January 2026: CBN upgraded OPay, Moniepoint, PalmPay, Kuda, Paga and others to national licences. Officials linked the move to tighter oversight. [N6]
- July 2026: CBN revoked 46 MFB licences, which created partner-bank risk for fintechs. [N12]
- Fintech executives describe regulators who "just show up and look at our books". [N7]

## 2. Cost and team pressure
- After the 2024 ban, full KYC checks reportedly rose from about NGN 400 to 700 per customer to NGN 1,500 to 2,000. [N8]
- Fintechs raised compliance hiring and poached experienced staff from banks. Kuda hired compliance analysts and a NIBSS compliance manager. [N8]
- A survey reported in a CBN-related piece: "87.5% of respondents report that the cost of meeting regulatory and risk requirements significantly impacts their capacity to innovate." The date and survey base are unconfirmed. [N14]
- No public salary benchmarks for fintech CCOs in Nigeria were found.

## 3. Market size
- 84 licensed payment service providers at end of 2025, including 8 MMOs (secondary, attributed to the CBN 2025 Annual Report, unverified). [secondary; the list is at N13]
- 16 switching and processing licensees (undated Nairametrics figure). [secondary]

## 4. Partner bank (sponsor bank) dynamics
- There's no standard partner-bank DDQ in Nigeria. Common asks are AML/KYC controls, security posture, licence and governance, and settlement documents. [N12, N23]
- The CBN is moving toward one onboarding and due-diligence baseline across regulated institutions. [N23]
- After the MFB revocations, fintechs are being told to diligence their partner banks too, which means more two-way questionnaires. [N12]

## 5. Competitive landscape (Nigeria-relevant)

### Local ID, KYC and KYB data vendors (likely integrations, the "your ID vendor" layer)
- **Youverify**: KYC, KYB (CAC registry), AML, transaction monitoring. Reported 800 active clients (TechCrunch, undated). [N16]
- **Dojah** (YC W22, Lagos): aggregated KYC, KYB, AML, biometrics. Lists OPay, FCMB, Cowrywise as customers. [N17]
- **Prembly** (Identitypass), **VerifyMe**, **Smile ID**: ID verification. Little KYB depth was found.

### Local AML platforms (closest direct competitors for monitoring)
- **Smartcomply / Adhere**: AI AML, KYC and fraud. BVN and NIN checks. Modular pricing. Company-reported "70 per cent reduction in manual compliance workload" and "40 per cent drop in false fraud alerts". Expanded to the UK. Positioned against the CBN baseline standards. [N15, N24]
- **Youverify** (above) also sells transaction monitoring.
- Also seen: Rulebase (compliance and back-office automation, raised USD 2.1m), Grace AI Lab (AI for banks, tied to the March 2026 mandate), Optimus AI Labs. Not yet assessed.

### Global AI compliance agents (same category as Asterisk, mostly US-focused)
- **Greenlite** (YC): AI agents for alert triage, risk scoring and TM reviews; raised $15m. [N18, N19]
- **Parcha**: AI agents for KYC and KYB reviews, including UBO screening. [N22]
- **Hummingbird**: AI agents for alert triage, case escalation and bank partner referrals. [N20]
- **Sardine**: three compliance AI agents, including sanctions screening. [N21]
- None was found marketing specifically to Nigeria.
